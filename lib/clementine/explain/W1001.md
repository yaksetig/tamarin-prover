# W1001: authentication() is aliveness only

## Summary

The `authentication(A, B)` query in your `verify` block compiles
to a Lowe *aliveness* lemma — the weakest form in Lowe's
authentication hierarchy. It only asserts that `B` ran the protocol
at some point if `A` thinks it did. It does NOT prove that `A` and
`B` agreed on any payload, or that distinct sessions of `A`
correspond to distinct sessions of `B`.

## Why this matters

Many users reach for `authentication()` when they actually want
`injective_agreement()`. The two prove substantially different
things. An aliveness lemma can hold for protocols that are
trivially insecure under stronger authentication models (e.g.
protocols vulnerable to UKS attacks).

## Recommendation

For mutual authentication of a key exchange, use:

```clem
injective_agreement(A, B, [k])
injective_agreement(B, A, [k])
```

For weaker properties, in increasing strength:

* `authentication(A, B)` — aliveness (W1001 fires)
* `non_injective_agreement(A, B, [...])` — non-injective agreement
* `injective_agreement(A, B, [...])` — full Lowe injective agreement
